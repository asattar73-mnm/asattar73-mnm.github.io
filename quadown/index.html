Support + Accountability = Ownership.

Support without accountability builds dependence. Accountability without support builds compliance out of fear. Leaders who give both get teachers who seek help, act on it and own their own improvement.

The four quadrants

Each quadrant is defined by what the leader gives, and each one teaches teachers a different habit. The same leader can be in Ownership with one department and Dependence with another, so apply QuadOwn teacher by teacher.

What support and accountability mean

Accountability is not punishment

QuadOwn uses accountability in the sense Fullan, Rincón-Gallardo and Hargreaves (2015) call internal accountability: teachers taking personal, professional and collective responsibility for the continuous improvement and success of all students. External checks work only when this inner responsibility is there (Elmore, 2000). The Oz Principle (Connors, Smith & Hickman, 1994) adds that accountability is a personal choice to show the ownership needed to get results.

Being held to account means the teacher agrees what they will improve, by when and how it will be shown, and the leader comes back to look at it with them. It never means blame, sanctions or catching people out.

Support builds capacity

  • DSDiagnosed support. Matches a need shown by evidence, not a general workshop.
  • CBCapacity building. The leader models, coaches and asks; the teacher does the fixing.
  • OROn request. The teacher knows who to ask and gets a quick response.

Accountability follows up

  • CEClear expectations. The teacher knows the standard, the target and how it will be checked.
  • ACAgreed commitments. Every conversation ends with an action, a date and the evidence.
  • FUFollow-up on impact. The leader returns on the date and checks the effect on students.

Where it tips over: when the leader fixes the plan instead of coaching (no CB), support builds dependence. When follow-up comes without matched support (no DS), accountability turns into inspection.

Moving a teacher to Ownership

Add what is missing; never take away what is already there.

FromAddFirst moves for the leader
DependenceAccountabilityStop fixing. Agree one commitment with a date and the evidence, then come back on that date. Answer "what should I do?" with "what have you tried, and what will you try next?"
ComplianceSupportPair every follow-up with matched help: a model lesson, a coaching cycle, time with a colleague. Make it safe to say "this isn't working yet".
DriftBoth, in orderSet clear expectations, diagnose the need and offer support, then agree the first commitment. Passing through Compliance or Dependence on the way is progress.
OwnershipKeep both, lighterHand the teacher more of the target-setting and follow-up. Use them to support colleagues. Check in less often, without stopping.

Related work

QuadOwn is my framework, drawn from my work leading teachers. Several published ideas sit close to it. Daloz (1986) crossed support with challenge in mentoring: support alone confirms, challenge alone causes retreat, and both together produce growth. The social discipline window (Wachtel & McCold, 2001) crosses support with control and names the low-low corner neglect. Elmore's (2000) reciprocal accountability holds that every demand for better performance must come with the capacity to meet it. Fullan (2011) argues that accountability used as the main driver of reform tends to fail without capacity building.

What QuadOwn adds: outcomes named as teacher behaviour (dependence, compliance out of fear, ownership, drift), the ownership cycle, and a checklist for leaders.

Limitation: the quadrants and the cycle come from my experience and testing in schools and have not yet been tested in a published study. Evidence from your own school (who asks for support, follow-up records, changes in student results) is the best check.
