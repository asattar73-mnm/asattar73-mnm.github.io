LeadCTRM · by Dr. A.Sattar Amri

From a clear goal to a team that owns it.

LeadCTRM is a four-stage framework for school leaders who need a team to reach a goal and keep reaching it. Make the goal clear, build the capacity, provide the resources, and grow the mindset that keeps people going when the work gets hard.

The four stages

Each stage asks one question. If the answer is no, fix that stage before moving on.

Stage 1 · the what

C Clarity

Does everyone know what we're aiming for, how we'll measure it, and who owns each part?

  • IGAn impact goal: a change for students, not an activity for staff
  • KPIBaseline, target, date and data source, with a leading KPI
  • BIThe team shapes the goal before it is fixed
  • OWOne named owner per KPI
Stage 2 · capacity

T Training

Can they do what the goal needs?

  • GAFind the real gap with evidence, and train only for it
  • MCModel, practise with feedback, coach in classrooms
  • EMHand over decisions as capacity grows
Stage 3 · support

R Resources

Do they have what the task needs, including time?

  • TMTools, materials and data ready before work starts
  • PTTime on the timetable, with something removed to make room
  • OSOngoing support: check-ins and quick action on barriers
Most important
Stage 4 · drive

M Mindset and motivation

Do they want to, and do they believe they can improve?

  • GMStretch targets; setbacks discussed openly; praise for strategy
  • IMAutonomy, mastery and purpose
  • RCSpecific recognition: the person, the practice, the effect

How a LeadCTRM cycle runs

Use one order to build a new initiative and another to diagnose one that is stalling.

Building

C→T→R→M

Make the goal clear, build capacity, put the support in place, then work deliberately on mindset and motivation.

Diagnosing

C→R→T→M

Check Clarity first: it is the most common gap and the cheapest to fix. Check Resources before Training, because people who could do the work but lack time won't be helped by more training. Look at mindset last, so you don't blame people for problems the system created.

M is also a layer across C, T and R

In ClarityThe goal is framed as a challenge worth taking on, and the team helps set it.
In TrainingMistakes during practice are expected and discussed; coaching is about growth, not judgment.
In ResourcesSupport is offered as investment in people, not as supervision.

Related work

LeadCTRM is my framework for school leaders. It builds on several established lines of research. Gilbert (1978) and Mager and Pipe (1997) showed that most performance gaps come from unclear expectations, missing tools or weak incentives rather than a lack of skill, and LeadCTRM's diagnosing order draws on their work. Goal-setting research found that specific, challenging goals, combined with commitment, beat vague "do your best" goals (Locke & Latham, 2002). Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan, 2000) and growth mindset research (Dweck, 2006) support Stage 4.

What LeadCTRM adds is one sequence designed for school leaders, separate orders for building and diagnosing, the codes and twenty look-fors, and mindset treated both as a stage and as a layer across the other three.

Limitation: growth mindset findings come mainly from studies of students. There is less evidence that mindset work changes teachers' practice, so Stage 4 relies partly on leadership experience.
