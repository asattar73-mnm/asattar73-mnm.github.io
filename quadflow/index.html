The four stages

Every QuadFlow lesson moves through four stages, with the productive task at its heart.

1

Learning Agreement

Do students know what they will learn and what success looks like?

Learning objective, success criteria, key vocabulary and a common understanding of the task.

2

Learning Support

Do students get support when they need it, and only then?

Enabling activities and short explicit teaching, triggered by students' stuck points.

3

Learning Product

Are students spending most of the lesson on the productive task?

Prior knowledge merged with new learning to create a product, in a real-life scenario.

4

Learning Assessment

Does the product show what students have learned?

Success criteria, self, peer and group assessment, and feedback that refines the product.

How a QuadFlow lesson runs

A stuck point is the evidence that support is needed. Students move to support, then straight back to the task.

Starting point

Linear model

Agreement → Support → Product → Assessment

Can help novice teachers start, but it has less impact. It risks teaching what students already know, and running out of time before the product is finished.

The target

Transitional model

Agreement → Product ⇄ Support → Assessment

Has more impact: students stay on the product and go to support only when they are stuck.
